## Plain English

The challenge asks for a continuous group homomorphism [Jacobian](#/node/Jacobian) X → Jacobian Y attached to every analytic f : X → Y of compact Riemann surfaces (`Jacobian/Challenge.lean` lines 106–108). `Jacobian.pushforward` supplies it (`Jacobian/Solution.lean` lines 132–136):

```lean
ULiftContinuousAddMonoidHom' (analyticPushforwardU f hf)
```

All the content is in [analyticPushforwardU](#/node/TraceDegree.analyticPushforwardU): write the pullback of holomorphic 1-forms f\* : H⁰(Y, Ω¹) → H⁰(X, Ω¹) as a matrix in the chosen bases, transpose it to get a map ℂ^{g_X} → ℂ^{g_Y}, and prove that the transpose carries the period lattice of X into the period lattice of Y so that it descends to the tori.

## Why the wrapper is needed

`Jacobian X` is `ULift.{u}` of a torus (`Jacobian/Solution.lean` lines 74–77). The torus itself lives in `Type 0` for every X, because its carrier is `Fin g → ℂ`. The specification demands `Jacobian X : Type u` when `X : Type u`, so the `ULift` is there purely to raise the universe. [ULiftContinuousAddMonoidHom'](#/node/ComplexTorus.ULiftContinuousAddMonoidHom') carries a homomorphism across the lift with source and target lifted independently, which is what lets X and Y sit in unrelated universes.

Applying `pushforward f hf` to a point means: strip the lift with `.down`, apply the torus map, put the lift back with `ULift.up`.

## How to think about it

A point of J(X) is a vector of numbers, one per basis form of X, modulo periods. Think of it as "a rule for integrating forms of X". To turn it into a rule for integrating forms of Y, pull the Y-form back to X and use the old rule. The change-of-variables identity `∫_{f∘γ} η = ∫_γ f*η` says this agrees with the geometric description: push the chain forward along f and integrate there. That is the identity the Lean actually uses, and it is what makes the map respect the two period lattices (`Jacobian/TraceDegree/AnalyticPushforwardU.lean` lines 101–119). Compatibility with the Abel–Jacobi map itself is not proved: the project states no lemma of the form `pushforward f hf (ofCurve P Q) = ofCurve (f P) (f Q)`.

## How it is used

Together with [pullback](#/node/Jacobian.pullback), it is the subject of `pushforward_pullback`: pushing forward after pulling back is multiplication by `ContMDiff.degree f hf`, the weighted fibre count of f. That is the fourth anti-hack check in the challenge.
