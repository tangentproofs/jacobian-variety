## Plain English

A space is Hausdorff when any two different points sit in disjoint open sets. It is the basic sanity condition that makes limits unique, and the challenge requires it of the Jacobian (Jacobian/Challenge.lean lines 72–73).

For a quotient ℂ^g / Λ this can fail. Take Λ ⊂ ℂ generated by 1 and √2. It is dense in the real line, so it is not closed, and the class of 0 in the quotient is not a closed point: the quotient is not even T1. What prevents this is that Λ is a *closed* subset of ℂ^g.

The Lean reflects that structure exactly. The instance itself is one line (Jacobian/Solution.lean line 96):

```
instance : T2Space (Jacobian X) := inferInstanceAs (T2Space (ULift _))
```

which hands off to the torus layer, where `quotient_t2Space` introduces the lattice's `isClosed` field and lets Mathlib's machinery do the rest: a closed subgroup gives a T1 quotient, and a T1 topological group is T2 (Jacobian/ComplexTorus/Defs.lean lines 82–88).

## Where closedness comes from

For the period lattice in particular, closedness is not assumed independently. The bundle fills that field using `AddSubgroup.isClosed_of_discrete` (Jacobian/Periods/GenuinePeriodLatticeDiscrete.lean line 83), so it rests on the discreteness theorem [periodLattice_discrete](#/node/Periods.periodLattice_discrete). Discreteness, in turn, is the hard analytic input: for closed chains γ and δ, the imaginary part of a certain period integral lies in πℤ, and the resulting real bilinear form is non-degenerate (Jacobian/Periods/GenuinePeriodLatticeDiscrete.lean lines 21–28).

So of the chain Hausdorff ← closed ← discrete, only the last link is real mathematics about Riemann surfaces. The first two are general topology.
