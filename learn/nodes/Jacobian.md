## Plain English

For a compact Riemann surface `X`, the holomorphic 1-forms on `X` form a finite-dimensional complex vector space, and its dimension is the genus `g` (see [genus](#/node/genus)). Integrating a form around a closed curve gives a number; doing it for a whole basis of forms gives a vector in ℂ^g. The subgroup of ℂ^g generated by all such vectors is a discrete grid: the **period lattice**. The Jacobian is ℂ^g with that grid collapsed — a compact complex torus of dimension `g`.

The Lean says this directly (Jacobian/Solution.lean lines 74–77):

```
def Jacobian (X : Type u) [...] : Type u :=
  ULift.{u} (JacobianChallenge.ComplexTorus.quotient (Fin (genus X) → ℂ)
    (JacobianChallenge.Periods.periodFullComplexLatticeU X))
```

Reading it right to left: [periodFullComplexLatticeU](#/node/Periods.periodFullComplexLatticeU) is the period lattice bundled with the facts that make quotienting work; [quotient](#/node/ComplexTorus.quotient) divides ℂ^g by it; `ULift` carries the result into the universe the specification asks for.

## What the lattice really is

The lattice is not built from singular homology. It is built from *chart chains*: finite lists of straight segments, each living inside a ball in one coordinate chart, glued end to end. The integral of a form along such a chain is the sum of ordinary line integrals in coordinates. The **period vector** of a chain is the tuple of its integrals against the chosen basis `ω₁, …, ω_g` ([periodVector](#/node/Periods.periodVector), Jacobian/Periods/GenuinePeriodLattice.lean lines 60–61).

The lattice is then generated by the differences `periodVector l₁ - periodVector l₂` for any two chains running between the same two points ([periodGenerators](#/node/Periods.periodGenerators), Jacobian/Periods/GenuinePeriodLattice.lean lines 76–78). A difference of two paths from `P` to `Q` is the same as going out along one and back along the other: a loop. Closed chains come out as the special case where the second chain is empty.

This presentation is deliberate. It makes the statement "the Abel–Jacobi integral does not depend on the path, modulo periods" true by the very definition of the lattice, which is how the classical construction is phrased.

## Why it is a torus and not something degenerate

Two things have to hold for the quotient to be a genuine torus, and both are theorems here:

- the lattice spans ℂ^g over ℝ ([periodLattice_span](#/node/Periods.periodLattice_span), proved by a maximum-principle argument: a form all of whose integrals have path-independent real part is zero);
- the lattice is discrete, so the quotient has charts and is not collapsed ([periodLattice_discrete](#/node/Periods.periodLattice_discrete), proved via a Hodge-theoretic non-degenerate pairing).

Discrete plus spanning makes it a `ZLattice` in Mathlib's sense, which has a ℤ-basis and hence a compact fundamental domain, and that is what makes the quotient compact (Jacobian/Periods/GenuinePeriodLatticeDiscrete.lean lines 47–72). Closedness, the last thing the quotient needs, follows from discreteness. Every field of `FullComplexLattice` is then filled in by [genuinePeriodLattice](#/node/Periods.genuinePeriodLattice) (Jacobian/Periods/GenuinePeriodLatticeDiscrete.lean lines 80–87).

## Reading the name carefully

`Jacobian X` is a compact complex torus of dimension `g`, constructed from the honest periods of honest holomorphic 1-forms. What it is *not* is the coordinate-free object H⁰(X, Ω¹)*/H₁(X, ℤ). The ℂ^g is coordinates for the dual space with respect to an arbitrary chosen basis of forms, and the lattice is defined by chains rather than by homology classes. Those are the two places where the formal object and the textbook object are related by a translation that the Lean does not carry out.
