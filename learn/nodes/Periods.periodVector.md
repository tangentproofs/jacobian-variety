## Plain English

A [chain](#/node/Periods.IsChain) gives one complex number per holomorphic 1-form, namely [chainIntegral](#/node/Periods.chainIntegral). Properly speaking, a chain is a linear functional on the space H⁰(X, Ω¹) of holomorphic 1-forms. To get a concrete vector in ℂ^g out of that functional, you have to pick coordinates, and coordinates on the dual space come from a basis of forms.

That is what `periodVector` does (`Jacobian/Periods/GenuinePeriodLattice.lean` lines 56–61). Fix the project-wide basis ω₁, …, ω_g of [holomorphic 1-forms](#/node/HolomorphicForms.HolomorphicOneForm), where g is [analyticGenus](#/node/HolomorphicForms.analyticGenus) ℂ X, the complex dimension of that space. Then

  periodVector X l = (∫_l ω₁, …, ∫_l ω_g) ∈ ℂ^g.

The two facts proved immediately are the ones you would expect: the empty chain gives 0 (Jacobian/Periods/GenuinePeriodLattice.lean lines 63–65), and concatenating chains adds the vectors (Jacobian/Periods/GenuinePeriodLattice.lean lines 67–70). Both come straight from the corresponding facts about `chainIntegral`.

## Why the chosen basis is honest but worth knowing about

[holomorphicOneFormFinBasis](#/node/HolomorphicForms.holomorphicOneFormFinBasis) is not a natural basis. It is whatever `Module.finBasis` hands back, which ultimately calls `Module.Free.chooseBasis` (`Jacobian/HolomorphicForms/BasisAlignedDualEquiv.lean` lines 48–55). So ℂ^g here is not "the" dual of H⁰(X, Ω¹); it is the dual read in one arbitrary basis.

This matters for how to read the result. The subgroup of ℂ^g generated by periods depends on the basis, so the Jacobian is a copy of the classical object rather than the object itself. What the project proves about that subgroup — discreteness, spanning ℂ^g over ℝ, and the quotient being a compact complex Lie group — is of the kind that a change of basis, being a linear isomorphism, would carry across. The project does not formalize that comparison, so read the construction as "a model of the Jacobian in chosen coordinates".
