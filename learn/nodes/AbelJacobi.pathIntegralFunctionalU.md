## Plain English

Fix a base point P. For each Q, take the [chosen chain](#/node/AbelJacobi.chosenChain) from P to Q and read off its [period vector](#/node/Periods.periodVector): the g integrals of the basis forms along that chain (`Jacobian/AbelJacobi/PathIntegralFunctionalU.lean` lines 44–49). That is

  pathIntegralFunctionalU X P Q = (∫_P^Q ω₁, …, ∫_P^Q ω_g) ∈ ℂ^g,

along one particular path.

This object is not well behaved, and the Lean does not pretend otherwise. Change the chain and the vector changes. Move Q a little and the chosen chain can be completely different, so the vector can jump. Even at Q = P the value need not be zero, because the chosen chain from P to P is some closed chain and its integral is a period (Jacobian/AbelJacobi/PathIntegralFunctionalU.lean lines 62–68).

## What is proved, and what it is worth

Exactly one thing: for every chain l from P to Q, the difference between this vector and the period vector of l lies in the [period lattice](#/node/Periods.genuinePeriodSubgroup) (Jacobian/AbelJacobi/PathIntegralFunctionalU.lean lines 53–60). That is the statement "the Abel–Jacobi integral is well defined modulo periods", and it is immediate, because Λ was [defined](#/node/Periods.periodGenerators) as the subgroup generated by these very differences.

Being honest about this matters. The well-definedness lemma is cheap by design, so it is not evidence that the construction is right. What makes the construction right is that Λ is also small: it is [discrete](#/node/Periods.genuinePeriodSubgroup_discrete), and distinct points of X give distinct classes when the genus is positive. Those are separate theorems with real analytic content.

The pay-off comes one step up. Although nothing controls this vector as Q moves, its class in ℂ^g/Λ is a holomorphic map X → [the Jacobian](#/node/AbelJacobi.BasisAnalyticJacobianU). Locally the jumps are by lattice elements, and in the quotient they vanish.
